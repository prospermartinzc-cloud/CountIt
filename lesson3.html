🍃 Food Chain

Discover how energy moves from one organism to another in an ecosystem.

🌱 1. What is a Food Chain?

A food chain is a sequence of organisms through which food and energy are transferred from one organism to another.

Each organism in a food chain depends on another organism for food.

For example:

🌱 Grass → 🐇 Grasshopper → 🐸 Frog → 🐍 Snake

This shows who eats whom and the direction in which energy is transferred.

☀️ 2. Producers

A producer is an organism that can make its own food.

Green plants are the main producers in many ecosystems because they use sunlight to manufacture food through photosynthesis.

Examples of producers include grass, maize plants and algae.

🐛 3. Consumers

A consumer is an organism that obtains food by feeding on other organisms.

Consumers cannot make their own food in the way producers do.

Animals are examples of consumers.

🐇 4. Primary Consumers

A primary consumer is a consumer that feeds directly on producers.

Primary consumers are usually herbivores.

Examples include grasshoppers, rabbits, goats and cattle.

Example:

🌱 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit

The rabbit is the primary consumer because it feeds directly on the grass.

🐍 5. Secondary Consumers

A secondary consumer is an organism that feeds on primary consumers.

Secondary consumers may be carnivores or omnivores.

For example:

🌱 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit → 🦊 Fox

The fox is a secondary consumer because it feeds on the rabbit.

🦅 6. Tertiary Consumers

A tertiary consumer feeds on secondary consumers.

Tertiary consumers may occur near the top of a food chain.

For example:

🌱 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit → 🦊 Fox → 🦅 Eagle

In this example, the eagle is a tertiary consumer because it feeds on the secondary consumer.

🦠 7. Decomposers

Decomposers are organisms that break down dead organisms and organic matter.

Examples include many bacteria and fungi.

Decomposers help return nutrients to the environment so that they can be used again by plants.

➡️ 8. Food-Chain Arrows

The arrow in a food chain shows the direction of energy transfer.

For example:

🌱 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit

The arrow points from the grass to the rabbit because energy is transferred from the grass to the rabbit when the rabbit feeds.

Therefore, the arrow does not simply mean "is eaten by"; it shows the direction of energy transfer.

⚡ 9. Energy Transfer

The main source of energy for most ecosystems is the Sun.

Green plants capture light energy and use it during photosynthesis.

When an animal eats a plant, some of the energy stored in the plant is transferred to the animal.

At each stage of a food chain, much energy is lost to the environment, including as heat during life processes.

🌍 10. Example of a Food Chain

Consider this food chain:

☀️ Sun → 🌱 Grass → 🦗 Grasshopper → 🐸 Frog → 🐍 Snake → 🦅 Eagle

Grass is the producer.

The grasshopper is the primary consumer.

The frog is a secondary consumer.

The snake is a tertiary consumer in this example.

The eagle is a higher-level consumer that feeds on the snake.

This food chain shows how energy is transferred through different feeding levels.
