Lesson 3

🧪 Investigating Photosynthesis

Scientists can carry out experiments to find out whether photosynthesis has taken place in a leaf.

🌿 Testing a Leaf for Starch

During photosynthesis, plants produce glucose. Some of the glucose is converted into starch and stored in the leaf.

Therefore, testing a leaf for starch can provide evidence that photosynthesis has occurred.

🧪 The Starch Test

Step 1 — Boil the leaf in water

The leaf is placed in boiling water for a short time. This kills the leaf and stops chemical reactions.

Step 2 — Remove the chlorophyll

The leaf is heated in alcohol using a water bath. The alcohol removes the green chlorophyll from the leaf.

The leaf becomes pale, making the colour change with iodine easier to observe.

Step 3 — Add iodine solution

Iodine solution is added to the leaf.

🧠 Important:

Iodine turns blue-black when starch is present.

If starch is absent, the iodine remains yellow-brown.

☀️ Is Light Necessary?

A plant can be kept in darkness for some time to remove stored starch. This is called destarching.

Part of a leaf is then covered with opaque material so that light cannot reach it.

The plant is placed in sunlight for several hours. The leaf is then tested for starch.

The part exposed to light turns blue-black, while the covered part does not.

This shows that light is necessary for photosynthesis.

🍃 Is Chlorophyll Necessary?

A variegated leaf contains both green and non-green areas.

After exposing the plant to sunlight, the leaf is tested for starch.

The green areas turn blue-black because they contain chlorophyll and have produced starch.

The non-green areas do not turn blue-black.

This provides evidence that chlorophyll is necessary for photosynthesis.

📚 WASSCE Reminder

Boiling water: kills the leaf and stops chemical reactions.

Alcohol: removes chlorophyll.

Iodine: tests for starch.

Blue-black colour: starch is present.

Destarching: removes stored starch.
