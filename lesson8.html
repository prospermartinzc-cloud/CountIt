Lesson 8

💧 Transport of Water and Mineral Salts

Plants need water and mineral salts for growth, photosynthesis and other life processes.

Water and mineral salts are absorbed from the soil by the roots and transported to different parts of the plant.

🌱 Absorption by Root Hairs

Root hairs are tiny extensions of root epidermal cells. They increase the surface area of the root for absorbing water and mineral salts.

Water enters root hair cells mainly by osmosis.

Mineral ions may enter the root by active transport.

🚰 The Xylem

The xylem is a type of vascular tissue that transports water and mineral salts from the roots towards the leaves and other parts of the plant.

Xylem vessels form long tubes through which water can move upwards.

⬆️ Movement of Water Up the Plant

Water moves from the roots through the xylem to the stem and leaves.

One important force helping this movement is the transpiration pull.

When water evaporates from the leaves during transpiration, it creates a pull that helps draw water upwards through the xylem.

🧂 Mineral Salts

Plants need mineral ions for healthy growth.

For example, nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids and proteins.

Magnesium ions are needed for the formation of chlorophyll.

🧠 WASSCE Reminder:

Root hairs → increase surface area for absorption.

Osmosis → movement of water across a partially permeable membrane.

Active transport → movement of substances against a concentration gradient using energy.

Xylem → transports water and mineral salts.

Transpiration pull → helps move water upwards through the xylem.
