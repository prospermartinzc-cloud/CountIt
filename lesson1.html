Biology as a Science of Life

Welcome to Lesson 1 of CountIt! In this lesson, we will learn what Biology is, what makes something living, and why Biology is important.

1. Biology as a Science of Life

Biology is the branch of science that studies living organisms. The word Biology comes from two Greek words: bios, meaning life, and logos, meaning study.

Therefore, Biology can be described as the scientific study of life and living organisms.

2. Biology as a Science

Biology is a science because it uses systematic methods to investigate living things.

Scientists observe organisms, ask questions, form hypotheses, carry out experiments, collect evidence and draw conclusions.

3. What is Life?

Life refers to the condition that distinguishes living organisms from non-living things.

Living organisms carry out processes such as nutrition, respiration, movement, growth, reproduction, excretion and sensitivity.

4. Characteristics of Living Organisms

A common way of remembering the characteristics of living organisms is MRS GREN.

M — Movement
Living organisms can move or show movement of parts of their bodies.

R — Respiration
Living organisms release energy from food through respiration.

S — Sensitivity
Living organisms detect and respond to changes in their environment.

G — Growth
Living organisms increase in size and undergo permanent changes.

R — Reproduction
Living organisms produce new organisms of their own kind.

E — Excretion
Living organisms remove waste products produced by their bodies.

N — Nutrition
Living organisms obtain or make food needed for energy, growth and repair.

5. Living and Non-living Things

Living things possess the characteristics of life.

Examples include humans, animals, plants, fungi and microorganisms.

Non-living things do not carry out all the life processes. Examples include stones, chairs, books and water.

6. How Biology is Studied

Biology can be studied through observation, experimentation, measurement, classification and careful recording of evidence.

Biological investigations should be carried out carefully so that reliable conclusions can be made.

7. Importance of Biology

Biology helps us understand how living organisms function and interact with their environment.

It contributes to medicine, agriculture, environmental protection, food production, biotechnology and many other areas of human life.

8. Careers Related to Biology

Biology can lead to many different careers.

Examples include medicine, nursing, pharmacy, veterinary medicine, biotechnology, microbiology, agriculture, environmental science and biological research.

9. Biology in Everyday Life

Biology is connected to everyday activities such as eating, breathing, exercising, farming, preventing diseases and caring for the environment.

Understanding Biology helps us make informed decisions about living organisms and our surroundings.

10. Why Study Biology?

Studying Biology helps us understand ourselves and the living world around us.

It also provides knowledge and skills that can be useful in education, careers, research, healthcare, agriculture and environmental conservation.
