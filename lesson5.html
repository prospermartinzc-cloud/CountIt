Lesson 5

🍃 Structure of a Leaf

A leaf is an important organ of a plant. It is mainly adapted for carrying out photosynthesis.

Different parts of a leaf have different structures that help the leaf perform its functions.

🌿 Main Parts of a Leaf

1. Waxy Cuticle

The waxy cuticle is a thin, waterproof layer covering the upper surface of the leaf.

It helps reduce water loss from the leaf.

2. Upper Epidermis

The upper epidermis is a layer of cells below the cuticle. It protects the inner tissues of the leaf.

3. Palisade Mesophyll

Palisade cells contain many chloroplasts. They are therefore well adapted for absorbing light and carrying out photosynthesis.

4. Spongy Mesophyll

The spongy mesophyll contains many air spaces. These spaces allow gases to move through the leaf.

5. Vascular Bundle

Vascular bundles contain xylem and phloem.

Xylem transports water and mineral salts to the leaf.

Phloem transports manufactured food such as sugars away from the leaf.

6. Stomata

Stomata are tiny pores found mainly on the lower surface of leaves.

They allow gases such as carbon dioxide and oxygen to move into and out of the leaf.

🔬 Guard Cells

Each stoma is surrounded by two guard cells.

Guard cells control the opening and closing of the stomata.

🧠 WASSCE Reminder:

Xylem → transports water and mineral salts.

Phloem → transports manufactured food.

Stomata → allow gaseous exchange.

Guard cells → control the opening and closing of stomata.

Palisade cells → contain many chloroplasts for photosynthesis.
