🔬 Cell Structure

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living organisms.

Although cells are very small, they contain structures that perform important functions that keep organisms alive.

1. What Is a Cell?

A cell is the smallest unit of an organism that can carry out the basic processes of life.

Some organisms consist of only one cell, while others are made up of many cells.

2. The Cell Membrane

The cell membrane surrounds the cell and separates the inside of the cell from its surroundings.

It controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

3. Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm is the jelly-like material found inside the cell.

Many chemical reactions needed for life take place in the cytoplasm.

4. The Nucleus

The nucleus contains genetic material and controls many activities of the cell.

It is often described as the control centre of the cell.

5. Mitochondria

Mitochondria are structures where aerobic respiration takes place.

They release energy from food for the cell's activities.

6. Ribosomes

Ribosomes are tiny structures involved in making proteins.

Proteins are important for growth, repair and many other functions in living organisms.

7. The Cell Wall

Plant cells have a cell wall outside the cell membrane.

The cell wall provides support and helps the plant cell keep its shape.

8. Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are found in plant cells and contain chlorophyll.

They are the main sites of photosynthesis in plant cells.

9. The Vacuole

Plant cells usually contain a large permanent vacuole.

The vacuole contains cell sap and helps maintain pressure inside the cell.

10. Plant Cells and Animal Cells

Plant and animal cells have several structures in common, including a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes.

Plant cells also have structures such as a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
