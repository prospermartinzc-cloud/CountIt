☀️ Photosynthesis

How green plants make their food using light energy.

🌱 1. What is Photosynthesis?

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food using light energy, carbon dioxide and water.

The food produced is mainly glucose. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

🔬 2. Where Does Photosynthesis Occur?

Photosynthesis mainly takes place in the leaves of green plants.

Inside the cells of the leaf are structures called chloroplasts.

Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that absorbs light energy.

☀️ 3. The Role of Light

Light provides the energy needed for photosynthesis.

Plants usually obtain this light from the Sun.

The chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs the light energy.

🌬️ 4. The Role of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is one of the raw materials needed for photosynthesis.

Plants obtain carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through tiny openings called stomata.

💧 5. The Role of Water

Water is another raw material needed for photosynthesis.

Plants absorb water from the soil through their roots.

The water is transported through the plant to the leaves.

🍃 6. The Role of Chlorophyll

Chlorophyll is the green pigment found inside chloroplasts.

Its major role in photosynthesis is to absorb light energy.

This energy is then used to drive the photosynthesis process.

🍬 7. Glucose

Glucose is the main food produced during photosynthesis.

Plants can use glucose immediately for energy through respiration.

Excess glucose can also be converted into starch and stored in different parts of the plant.

🌬️ 8. Oxygen

Oxygen is released during photosynthesis.

Some of the oxygen is used by the plant during respiration, while much of it can diffuse out into the atmosphere.

⚗️ 9. The Photosynthesis Equation

The word equation for photosynthesis is:

Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen

Light energy and chlorophyll are required for the process.

🌍 10. Why Photosynthesis Matters

Photosynthesis is extremely important because it provides food for plants and forms the starting point of many food chains.

It also releases oxygen into the atmosphere.

Animals and humans depend directly or indirectly on plants for food.
