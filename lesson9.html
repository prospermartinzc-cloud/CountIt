Lesson 9

🍬 Translocation of Manufactured Food

Plants manufacture food during photosynthesis, mainly in the form of glucose.

The glucose may be converted into other substances such as sucrose and transported to different parts of the plant.

🚚 What is Translocation?

Translocation is the movement of manufactured food substances from one part of a plant to another through the phloem.

Unlike xylem, which mainly transports water and mineral salts, phloem transports organic food substances.

🌿 The Phloem

Phloem is a vascular tissue found in the veins of leaves, stems and roots.

It transports substances such as sucrose from areas where they are produced or released to areas where they are needed or stored.

📍 Source and Sink

The part of the plant where food is produced or released for transport is called a source.

The part of the plant where food is used or stored is called a sink.

🧠 Example:

A mature leaf can act as a source because it produces food by photosynthesis.

A growing root can act as a sink because it uses transported food for growth and respiration.

🌱 Why is Translocation Important?

Translocation allows food to reach parts of the plant that cannot produce enough food by themselves.

The transported food can be used for:

• Growth

• Respiration

• Storage

• Production of new tissues and substances

📚 WASSCE Reminder:

Translocation → movement of manufactured food through the phloem.

Phloem → transports manufactured food substances.

Source → area where food is produced or released for transport.

Sink → area where food is used or stored.
