Lesson 7

💧 Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of a plant, especially through the stomata of the leaves.

🌿 Where Does Transpiration Occur?

Most transpiration occurs through the stomata on the leaves.

A smaller amount of water may also be lost through the cuticle and through openings called lenticels.

💨 How Does Transpiration Occur?

Water is absorbed from the soil by the roots and transported to the leaves through the xylem.

In the leaf, water evaporates from the surfaces of the mesophyll cells into the air spaces.

The water vapour then diffuses out of the leaf through the stomata.

🧠 Simple sequence:

Soil → Roots → Xylem → Leaf → Air spaces → Stomata → Atmosphere

☀️ Factors Affecting Transpiration

The rate of transpiration is affected by several environmental factors.

1. Temperature

Higher temperature generally increases the rate of transpiration because water evaporates more quickly.

2. Humidity

High humidity reduces the rate of transpiration because there is less difference in water vapour concentration between the leaf and the surrounding air.

3. Wind

Wind generally increases transpiration by removing water vapour from around the leaf.

4. Light intensity

Increased light intensity can increase transpiration because stomata tend to open to allow carbon dioxide to enter for photosynthesis.

🌱 Importance of Transpiration

Transpiration helps create a pull that assists the movement of water and mineral salts from the roots to the leaves.

It also helps cool the plant when water evaporates from the leaves.

📚 WASSCE Reminder:

Transpiration: loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of a plant.

Main site: stomata.

Water transport: xylem.

High temperature: generally increases transpiration.

High humidity: generally decreases transpiration.

Wind: generally increases transpiration.
