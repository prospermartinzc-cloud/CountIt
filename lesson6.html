Lesson 6

💨 Gas Exchange in Plants

Plants need to exchange gases with their surroundings in order to carry out important life processes.

The main gases involved are carbon dioxide and oxygen.

🍃 Gas Exchange Through Stomata

Most gas exchange in leaves takes place through tiny openings called stomata.

Carbon dioxide enters the leaf through the stomata and is used during photosynthesis.

Oxygen produced during photosynthesis can leave the leaf through the stomata.

🔬 Guard Cells

Each stoma is surrounded by two guard cells.

Guard cells control the opening and closing of the stomata.

When the stomata are open, gases can move into and out of the leaf more easily.

🌙 Stomata at Night

Many plants close their stomata at night because photosynthesis is not taking place without light.

However, the behaviour of stomata can vary between different plants.

🌱 Gas Exchange in Roots

Roots also need oxygen for aerobic respiration.

Oxygen from the air spaces in the soil enters the roots by diffusion.

Carbon dioxide produced during respiration diffuses out of the roots.

🧠 WASSCE Reminder:

Stomata → openings through which gases move in and out of leaves.

Guard cells → control stomatal opening and closing.

Carbon dioxide → enters the leaf for photosynthesis.

Oxygen → can leave the leaf after being produced during photosynthesis.

Roots → obtain oxygen from air spaces in the soil for respiration.
